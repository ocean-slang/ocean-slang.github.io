Satellites and airborne sensors reveal submesoscale (1–10 km) variability in ocean color in the form of filaments, eddies, and patches. The variability in ocean color is closely tied to the physical dynamics that restructure phytoplankton distributions and drive active biological responses like changes in primary productivity and community structure. As the base of the marine food web and a key component of the biological carbon pump, phytoplankton are crucial to the overall health of marine ecosystems and to the ocean’s role in climate.

Research region

California Current System

The California Current System, like other Eastern Boundary Upwelling Systems, contributes disproportionately to primary productivity in the ocean by upwelling cold, nutrient-rich waters that fuel surface phytoplankton growth and high POC concentrations. The cold, highly productive water is subsequently transported offshore through horizontal stirring by the geostrophic strain field resulting in subduction and mixing with warmer, oligotrophic waters further offshore. The intensity of horizontal stirring impacts mesoscale chlorophyll-a variability and the formation of upwelling fronts, which can become unstable and form submesoscale meanders and instabilities.

Research region

Gulf Stream

In the Gulf Stream, large energetic eddies peeling off the main current influence phytoplankton ecosystems in the Sargasso Sea. Following a cold-core Gulf Stream eddy, we diagnosed the impacts of horizontal mesoscale eddy dynamics on submesoscale phytoplankton patchiness and community structure by leveraging state-of-the-art satellite currents, hyperspectral data from the PACE satellite, and Lagrangian analysis.

North Brazil Current

The North Brazil Current (NBC) is a Western Boundary Current in the tropical Atlantic Ocean. When the NBC approaches the equator, the Coriolis parameter approaches 0 and the current is retroflected eastward, joining the North Equatorial Counter Current. The retroflection generates NBC rings, large anticyclonic eddies whose northwestard transport provides a key pathway for the northward return flow of the Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation. Rings can also entrain Amazon River water nutrients, increasing phytoplankton concentrations and carbon drawdown. The NBC also plays a role in offshore freshwater and carbon transport eastward, advecting the Amazon River Plume offshore and around the retroflection. Interactions between the strong shear of the NBC, fresh Amazon river water, and oligotrophic offshore waters generate meso- and submesoscale variability in phytoplankton and carbon, whose impacts are still being uncovered. Traditional satellite altimetry misses the finer scales of these dynamics, but new machine learning products are improving the retrieval of mesoscale and submesoscale currents from space.

High chlorophyll-a signatures captured by multispectral satellite algorithms (like shown below) overestimate chlorophyll-a at the coast and in the plume due to the presence of detritus and colored dissolved organic matter (CDOM). In reality, high sediment loads in river water close to the coast shield phytoplankton from sunlight, resulting in low productivity. As river water is transported offshore by the NBC, sediment sinks out of the water column and light availability increases, increasing net primary productivity. Still, in the offshore plume region, multispectral algorithms still significantly overestimate chlorophyll-a due to CDOM. Satellites predict chlorophyll-a concentrations of about 0.8 mg m−3 in the offshore plume (see imagery below), values are closer to 0.1 mg m−3 as measured in situ. Although standard ocean color chlorophyll-a products cannot distinguish between chlorophyll-a and detritus/organic matter, we can separate the signal using hyperspectral imagery from PACE and in situ sampling.
